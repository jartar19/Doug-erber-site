How I got here

From a classroom in Anjo to three decades of nonprofit leadership.

1989 · Anjo

My career in international relations and nonprofit leadership began in 1989, when I moved to Anjo, Japan, to teach English in public junior high schools and serve as the city’s International Coordinator. That experience gave me a deep appreciation for building meaningful relationships across cultures, and inspired me to pursue a career connecting people and communities.

1993 · JASSC

In 1993 I joined the Japan America Society of Southern California, where I spent the next 27 years, eventually serving as President and leading its fundraising, membership development, community partnerships and operations.

Since 2021

Since then I’ve served as an executive director and nonprofit board leader, helping organizations strengthen their financial sustainability, expand their reach and fulfill their missions.

Why

What has kept me passionate about this work for more than three decades is the opportunity to bring people together, build lasting relationships and make a meaningful difference in the communities we serve.

Leadership, fundraising and international partnerships · JASSC

$1.5 million Japan Disaster Relief Fund

After the devastating 2011 earthquake, tsunami and nuclear disaster in Japan, I created and managed a $1.5 million relief fund through the Japan America Society of Southern California. It supported immediate humanitarian relief and long-term recovery, and showed what individuals, businesses and communities can do together in a time of extraordinary need.

$250K
Major donor cultivation and relationship building · JASSC

$250,000 Centennial Celebration gift

As the Japan America Society prepared to celebrate its 100th anniversary, it needed significant support to present a meaningful, year-long Centennial Celebration. Through personal relationship building and donor cultivation, I secured a $250,000 contribution from one generous family to help make the milestone possible.

25% membership growth, 90% retention

To build a strong, sustainable membership base, I secured a $150,000, three-year capacity-building grant from the Japan Foundation’s Center for Global Partnership, then led a membership campaign that grew corporate and individual membership by 25% and reached a 90% renewal rate.
